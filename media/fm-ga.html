On the method

Every specimen carries a genome of fourteen genes, each a number between 0 and 1. The genes decode to the parameters of a small FM voice: a sine-wave modulator bends the frequency of a sine-wave carrier, feedback lets the modulator bend its own phase into a richer wave, vibrato wavers the carrier pitch, and a curved attack–release envelope shapes the amplitude. Parameters marked start → end glide between two values across the note. Frequencies map onto their genes exponentially, so blending two genomes blends their pitches in equal musical ratios. You are the fitness function. Specimens you rate highly are more likely to survive selection and pass their genes to the next generation, so the population adapts to your taste. Each drawing is computed from the genome, and identical genomes always produce identical figures.

Mutation

After crossover, each gene may change at random. Probability sets how often a gene mutates and sigma sets how far it moves.

Population

Size is the number of specimens in each generation. Elites are the top-rated specimens, copied unchanged into the next generation.
