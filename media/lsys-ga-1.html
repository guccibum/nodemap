On the method

Every specimen carries a genome in two parts. The first spells a grammar: for each of the eight symbols of the alphabet, a production rule of one to four symbols, decoded from continuous genes so that crossover can blend two grammars and mutation can drift one rule into a neighboring one. The second spells the draw rules of a small microsound engine — the windowed hard-sync oscillator — that reads the grammar's string one symbol at a time. Starting from the axiom p, the rules are rewritten generation upon generation, and the string is taken from however many rewritings it needs to sing for at least five seconds; the performance is cut at ten. Nothing is concatenated across rewritings, and unlike drifting rules inside a single string, evolution here happens between specimens: the genetic algorithm breeds whole grammars against each other.

Only p and . make time pass. The other six symbols nudge three hidden states, and the states wander without bound — but each is folded through a sine wave and mapped exponentially between the minimum and maximum written in the genome, so no frequency ever escapes its range, and every step through the fold moves in equal musical ratios rather than equal hertz. The frequency bounds sit on their genes exponentially too, so blending two genomes blends their registers in equal ratios. The rules for p and . are pinned to contain a p, so no lineage ever falls silent.

Each figure is drawn from the very same string by a second set of draw rules. The pen circles the plate as an unbroken line: its turning is tied to the sync state, its reach from the centre to the intrinsic state — folded through the same sine that bounds the frequencies, which is what keeps the line inside the plate — and its weight of ink to the amplitude state. Silences lift the pen and leave a gap. What you see is the sound's own trajectory, and while a specimen plays, the line is retraced in time with the audio. Identical genomes always produce identical figures and identical sound. You are the fitness function: specimens you rate highly are more likely to survive selection and pass both their grammar and their draw rules to the next generation, so the population adapts to your taste.

Beneath each figure, WAV saves the specimen's sound, PNG its finished figure at high resolution, GIF the retracing as a silent animation, and FILM a real-time movie of figure and sound together (keep the tab visible while it records). Files are named by generation, figure, and genome so no two downloads collide. SAVE in the header writes the whole session — population, ratings, and settings — into a JSON file; LOAD restores it exactly.

Mutation

After crossover, each gene may change at random. Probability sets how often a gene mutates and sigma sets how far it moves.

Population

Size is the number of specimens in each generation. Elites are the top-rated specimens, copied unchanged into the next generation.

Grammar

Every production rule is a word of min to max symbols, decoded from the genome. Genes beyond a rule's length lie dormant and can resurface when the length mutates.
